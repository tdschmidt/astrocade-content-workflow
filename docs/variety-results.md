## Discovery and editorial selection

The [hydrated catalog](../data/experiments/variety-2026-10-07/discovery-hydrated.json) contains 100 unique candidates balanced across public Trending, Top Picks and homepage discoveries. The model's initial nominations supplied capture goals, rejection conditions and provisional gameplay families. Later replacements were deliberately selected from observed candidates after failed probes; this batch is an iterative engineering exercise, not a single unattended ten-game run or a random benchmark. The [initial queue review](../data/experiments/variety-2026-10-07/selection-review.md) distinguishes title-based hypotheses from verified mechanics.

The [dated trend brief](../data/experiments/trends-2026-10-07.json) is context, not measured popularity: it records October seasonal gaming, adjacent-platform brainrot interest and secondary short-form format ideas, expiring October 10. Halloween has the clearest seasonal fit; most final premises are evergreen. Unlabeled Astrocade counters do not prove popularity, and Fortnite audience figures do not transfer to Astrocade or Reels. No “currently viral,” engagement uplift or optimal video-length claim is made.

Normal editing proposes three premises, selects verified source windows, then reviews actual source frames for causality, timing and overlay placement. `--from-run` creates a new edit referencing hashed sources and saved analysis; originals stay unchanged. Some separate context experiments explicitly reanalyzed the same source. Archer, Crowd, Melody and Sort pairs share cuts while changing premises; Rendang, Halloween and Iron Golem also vary the episode or duration. These are creative comparisons, not audience A/B results.

## Reliability and remaining limits

- **Input coverage is bounded.** Timed replay and sparse screenshot feedback use observed controls through native Playwright input. Pointer paths, left/right buttons and relative Mouse Look are general primitives, not hidden game APIs. Look requires visible control evidence, an opted-in profile, actual browser pointer lock and a known executor-owned cursor position. There are no game-clock changes or fabricated gameplay frames.
- **Slow feedback is not reflex control.** New feedback profiles allow at most 175s including inference, 16 decisions, eight actions/10s declared duration per batch, and up to 30s per decision. The last decision is evaluation-only; a one-second settling delay follows each batch. Old saved limits persist. Actual latency and stale observations can defeat moving hazards; the [design discussion](design-decisions.md#live-feedback-boundary-and-options-for-reflex-play) records alternatives.
- **Control success is not content success.** Minecraft35 placed four blocks but did not establish a well-framed recognizable construction; it stays excluded. Taming did not supply a usable captured episode. Brief marks, passive reveals, unreadable puzzle transitions and inadequately held text/results are not counted merely because a command or model assessment succeeded.
- **Visual inference remains fallible.** Models confused projectile ownership, focus with submitted choice, readable type with sufficient reading time, and available overlap with complete payoff. The latest fix merges overlapping verified observations locally before editing; gaps, per-window eligibility and saved analysis remain intact. A shortened final cut still needs its entire omitted tail reviewed and sufficient shown result time. Sampling and semantic judgment cannot guarantee comprehension.
- **Evidence is inspectable, not hidden reasoning.** Runs retain `report.md`, `run.json`, `trace.jsonl`, learned profiles, screenshots, native inputs, source hashes, analyses and edit decisions. Returned structured responses are saved before workflow validation with unique IDs and a 64 KiB bound; configured secrets are redacted. Prompts, media payloads and internal model reasoning are not logged by that wrapper. Provider-internal schema/transport failures have no returned object to retain.
- **Verification is scoped.** These silent portrait clips were tested locally on macOS; broad cross-platform capture, arbitrary games and repeatable audience performance are unproven. Publishing/account setup and a GUI are outside this narrowed handoff. The actual media must still be reviewed before manual publication.
