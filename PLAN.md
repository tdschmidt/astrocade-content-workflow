# Current plan: find games, learn to play, make gameplay reels

The deliverable is a small local TypeScript CLI that identifies suitable Astrocade games, learns their visible controls, records native gameplay, and edits useful footage into short portrait videos. Account creation and publishing are outside the current deliverable. No GUI, database, queue, deployment, or extra service is required. The previous broader implementation remains on `main`; [design decisions](docs/design-decisions.md) record the tradeoffs.

## What the workflow should produce

A run produces a finished MP4, posting copy, original footage, and an inspectable account of selection, observed controls, executed actions, results, and editing decisions. This trace contains concise decision summaries and evidence, not private internal reasoning. A completed process or a correct caption is insufficient: the video must make the game look worth playing.

The audience is Roblox/brainrot viewers and people who recognize Pokémon, Ben 10, cartoon/game nostalgia, or internet characters. Prefer a recognizable premise with several actual things to do. Hooks should sound like a gaming group chat: disbelief, playful trash talk, nostalgic investment, or an absurd crossover. Let the footage finish the joke. Avoid generic workplace metaphors and literal play-by-play. Fictional satire and expressive POV are allowed; unsupported real-person allegations, invented game outcomes, and sexual/minor jokes are not. [Audience research](docs/audience-reel-research.md) distinguishes historical examples from current trend evidence; the user's reference voice remains authoritative.

## Pipeline

| Stage | Approach | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find and nominate | Read public Astrocade catalog/pages and nominate up to three candidates by default, configurable from one to five. Prefer culturally recognizable games with plausible, varied gameplay rather than a satisfying skin on one repetitive action. An explicit public game URL can bypass catalog selection. | Retain source URLs, observed metadata, selection hypotheses, proposed capture goals, and rejection conditions. Unlabeled counters remain unknown. A nomination does not establish that a mechanic works. |
| Inspect and learn | Observe loading, native start/setup menus, the actual game surface, and visible help/tutorial controls. New reel runs default to `auto`: reuse a matching verified timed profile when no new duration is requested; otherwise assess screenshot feedback first, with timed fallback only when feedback is unsupported. Both assessments use the same inspection. | Save screenshots, instructions, bounded setup steps, learned controls, and limitations. Randomized puzzles can use fresh screenshots. Provider failures do not establish that a game is unsupported, and model confidence does not verify a profile. |
| Play and capture | Use Playwright native input and native tab recording in a fresh browser. Probe a control, practice promising mechanics, then use confirmed abilities toward visible goals: navigate, orient the camera, traverse, aim, interact, and explore new opportunities. A form-selection tour or a first animation is not sufficient gameplay. | New reel feedback profiles default to 600 seconds including inference latency, with 40 decisions; an explicit 175-second budget retains 16 decisions. `--capture-seconds 5-600` bounds new source capture. Saved budgets and verified timed presets remain unchanged. Stop on a terminal result, demonstrated block, exhausted budget, or broad useful coverage with no worthwhile next opportunity. |
| Observe and adapt | Compare the previous screenshot, up to six timestamped frames sampled during the preceding action batch, and the separate current screenshot. Maintain up to eight mechanics with `untried`, `testing`, `working`, or `blocked` status, visible evidence, and a next goal. | A late landed frame does not negate earlier visible flight. Ability animation does not prove a hit or win. Only the current frame supplies current coordinates. All keys must have observed control evidence; simultaneous input supports two or three unique allowed keys. Inputs release after each action. |
| Analyze | Inspect the full source at 1 FPS in bounded windows, then selected moments at 8 FPS. Use saved action-time observations as search hints for brief effects; independent dense video review must confirm them. Seek purposeful gameplay, local cause/effect, and feature variety; retain only supported source intervals. Longer source recording is separate from final video length. | Preserve observations, source timestamps, uncertainty, and rejected moments. No invented targets, scores, consequences, or context beyond the observed footage. Hints never become event evidence on their own, and legacy recordings still support visual scouting. |
| Edit and render | The unified CLI selects meme (15–25s), narrated overview (roughly30–45s), or grounded story over progression footage (roughly35–60s). Fresh phonk edits rank the true climax, align visual/audio treatment, and retain at least4s of payoff including2s moving aftermath. Overviews explain premise/choices/appeal; story backgrounds receive independent progression review. | Reuse the finished editorial package, real catalog audio, measured speech timestamps and deterministic720×1280/30FPS renderers. Preserve plans, source hashes, failed attempts and offscreen credits. Legacy silent highlights remain available explicitly. |

After the initial single-control probe, feedback should use safe, goal-directed batches of roughly four to eight seconds when appropriate. Reel batches allow up to 32 existing native actions within ten seconds of declared action time; legacy episodes retain eight. This lets confirmed clicker loops reach visible combo rewards while preserving pauses between taps, without broadening speculative menu actions. Single keys and simultaneous keys can hold for up to six seconds; pointer paths and relative look remain bounded to two seconds. Relative look requires both observed Mouse Look instructions and actual browser pointer lock. Menus, uncertain inputs, or hazards justify shorter actions.

This is screenshot feedback, not a reflex controller. The game continues during inference. Samples reduce mistaken conclusions about transient effects, but sparse views and model latency still limit combat, fast targeting, and timing-sensitive play. The checklist is model memory backed by saved observations, not an independent proof of mastery.

For a confirmed stable clicker target, reel feedback can use compact native `taps`: 2–40 clicks over 200–6000ms, at least 100ms per click. Test a short cadence and compare actual rewards/counts before scaling; slow down if the game drops inputs. Each click releases before the next interval, cancellation stops the remaining sequence, and the declared duration counts toward the same ten-second batch limit. This is unavailable for the first probe or legacy planner requests.

## Architecture and recovery

Use ordinary TypeScript functions, Zod boundaries, Playwright, FFmpeg, and either Gemini or the authenticated Codex CLI provider. Gemini requires a configured key. Codex uses the existing ChatGPT login and rejects API-key authentication for this workflow. Provider requests are bounded; persistent failures surface with saved progress. No new agent framework or external infrastructure is needed.

The CLI supports staged discovery/capture/editing, direct game selection, provider/model choice, resume, and source reuse. See [README](README.md) for commands. Each run owns a local directory and a write lock. Save source recordings and hashes, control profiles, sampled action images, structured proposals, feedback reports, analysis, edit choices, and final media as work completes. Current evidence belongs in `report.md`, `trace.jsonl`, and the saved JSON/image artifacts.

`--resume` reuses completed artifacts and their recorded provider/model; overrides affect unfinished work. A saved script resumes rendering without repeating capture or analysis. `--from-run` creates a separate edit of unchanged saved footage and inherits its brief unless a replacement is supplied. Analysis is reused only when the saved `analysisEditingStyle` matches the requested style. Old briefs without an editing style remain legacy episode edits, including their 40-second upper limit. Changing an old source to a reel brief reanalyzes it; it does not alter the original recording or run. Hash checks reject changed artifacts.

Capture-budget expiration releases inputs and finalizes the source. Later controller-inference failures preserve already played footage with an explicit error. Optional action-frame sampling must not discard otherwise valid gameplay when a sample fails. Operator cancellation cleans up owned resources and preserves completed artifacts. Every failed or partial run remains distinguishable from a successful gameplay or editorial result.

## Acceptance and current status

The latest three-format workflow is integrated into the main CLI. Retired GUI/account/publishing components have been removed, and two fresh ten-minute capture-to-video runs plus the continued category batch exercise the integrated path. See the [current validation report](docs/pipeline-validation-2026-10-07.md) and [checkpoint](CHECKPOINT.md) for selected candidates, remaining review limits and exact local evidence. The paragraphs below record earlier milestones, not the current output selection.

Earlier runs established discovery, native input/capture, source reuse, portrait rendering, and truthful editing across several games. The [21-video functional batch](docs/variety-results.md) is historical system evidence; it was not approved as the current humor or gameplay standard.

The earlier approximately 8.1-second Ben 10 reel was rejected as insufficient gameplay. Two fresh captures exercised the improved controller; the second recorded 431.832 seconds and independently demonstrated street movement, a fire projectile, and sustained combined movement/flight across city blocks. The editor recovered the brief projectile through action-observation hints and produced multi-shot reels from unchanged source footage. See [the gameplay improvement report](docs/ben10-gameplay-results.md) for the selected render, comparisons, exact evidence, tests and failures. No target hit or completed navigation route was established; the agent correctly stopped after losing the route and failing to recover it.

Future changes retain the same acceptance standard: review both source and rendered reel. Look for purposeful camera/route traversal, a demonstrated mechanic reused toward a visible goal, and a distinct interaction or consequence. Do not equate a transformation, an animation, or an unchanged health bar with successful combat. The final reel should make these actual playable features understandable and entertaining, with a natural, culturally apt hook. Tests and valid files establish the implementation boundaries; actual playback establishes whether it satisfies the assignment.
