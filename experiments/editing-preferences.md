# Shared editing preferences

User feedback, 2026-10-07. Apply to these separate editing experiments and carry forward when integrating them.

- Let the action develop. Aim for roughly 15–25 seconds for a meme edit and 30–45 seconds for a narrated overview when the source supports it. Story retellings may run 35–60 seconds. Do not stretch a weak four-second event to hit a quota: select a richer source with a real setup, action and consequence.
- Use actual familiar meme sound clips and real music assets from the reviewed catalog. Procedural demonstration tones are not substitutes. Keep source, author, downloaded-file hash, licensing evidence and exact-use limits in the asset manifest. Unknown rights stay labeled preview-only; public availability is not a royalty-free license.
- Attach a reaction face to a real visible head. For a freeze, record its source-frame box and the final output placement after every crop and zoom, then inspect the rendered frame. For motion, supply reviewed tracking keyframes throughout the visible interval; otherwise omit the sticker. A floating decorative face does not satisfy face replacement.
- Add no author-credit, game-name, logo or other watermark to the video. Keep credits on the review page and in metadata. Native gameplay HUD can remain when it conveys mechanics; remove app chrome when the reviewed crop permits it.
- An overview explains what the game is: its premise, player role, core loop/objective when known, meaningful choices and appeal. Ground it in the whole gameplay plus sourced game description. Relevant pictures support that narrative without a one-to-one sentence/action match. Reject frame-by-frame movement commentary even when accurate. The story-background format can use unrelated gameplay.
- Choose the main meme climax before placing music. Rank observed events by their consequence in the story, not just movement or volume. Save the largest visual change and phonk drop for the decisive action late in the edit, with enough aftermath to understand it and enjoy the drop. Aim for roughly 4–6 seconds after the main drop, ending on a resolved musical phrase over real aftermath; fresh phonk jobs require at least 4 seconds including 2 seconds of moving footage. Do not move the climax to a weaker moment, pad a freeze or add idle footage to meet that floor. A smaller survival/reaction moment can build toward that payoff but must not exhaust the edit's strongest treatment. Record the exact event frame and its source-to-output timing.
- Start game overviews with a specific, evidence-backed hook: an unusual player choice, immediate stakes, a surprising mechanic, or a question the footage will answer. Use an opening picture relevant to that promise; every premise-level fact need not occur in the first shot. Concrete ‘in this game you can…’ phrasing is fine; the idea must earn attention. A generic recommendation or a list of tools is not an adequate hook. Retention is an editorial objective, not a measured result without audience data.
- For new overview samples, compare several top-game recordings and favor footage with demonstrated choices, distinct mechanics, progression or a meaningful objective. Use real ranking evidence when calling a game popular; do not infer depth from its title or menus.
- Narration backgrounds need sustained, satisfying progression and changing situations. Either one good continuous run or multiple coherent sections is valid. Reject repeated failed attempts at the same obstacle even when deaths are hidden or source frames differ. Prefer accessible running, dodging, collecting or traversal when parkour control is unreliable; do not salvage a retry loop just to cover the voice.
- Keep all current previews together on one review page. Prior versions may be kept separately for comparison and must be clearly labeled as earlier drafts.

These preferences do not authorize publishing or sending the videos externally.
