# Background gameplay selector

Select original gameplay for an unrelated narrated story or information segment. The voice is the primary content, but the picture must be satisfying in its own right: sustained progress, changing obstacles, successful dodges, collection, route choices or other readable little payoffs. Parkour is optional. Consider runners, obstacle dodging, collecting, driving or accessible traversal when reflex-heavy platforming does not produce a good run. A Subway Surfers-like runner is a format reference, not a claim about the source game or permission to use someone else's recording.

Inspect actual motion throughout the proposed selection, including every cut and what happened before/after each source excerpt. A good continuous run works. So does a coherent montage of satisfying sections. Neither continuity nor a large cut count is inherently a virtue. Choose based on changing situations and progression, not file length, genre label or total unique frames.

Reject repeated attempts at the same failed section EVEN IF the falls, deaths, respawns and resets have been removed. Different files, fresh timestamps and nonoverlapping source intervals can still show the same start-platform approach over and over. Identify recurring landmarks across attempts. Do not turn fragments of failed attempts into a nominally successful montage. An isolated clean excerpt from an attempt that later fails is usable if it has a satisfying endpoint; repeatedly harvesting the same obstacle is not.

Reject loading, menus, shops, long input/inference pauses, stalled movement, repeated failure/reset, prolonged static results, rapid camera spins, occlusion and featureless wandering. Motion alone is insufficient. Do not assume success or visual interest from a game's title, screenshots of a jump, or an earlier highlight's acceptance. State what changes, what the player accomplishes and why there is something to watch throughout.

Read the target voice duration. Supply enough interesting normal-speed footage for the narration and ending. Never repeat a short clip, freeze, fabricate gameplay, or greatly slow it to fill the voice. If coverage is weak, choose a better source/game or shorten the script before generation. After a bounded probe repeatedly fails the same obstacle, stop salvaging retries and consider a game with less demanding controls. Fresh capture requires the active task's authorization; a proposed route is not a successful capture.

Return source path/hash, source in/out, actual duration, timestamped motion evidence, crop/caption-safe area, exclusions, continuity classification and alternatives. Record audio presence from probing, not images. Preserve essential character, obstacle and HUD information; no extra author/game watermarks.

A selection for prepare-background.ts must include progressionReview with: decision (accept/reject), watchedWholeSelection, engagementSummary, repeatedFailureLoop, and spans. Each span has start/end in the proposed OUTPUT timeline, sectionId, attemptId, outcome (progress/retry/failure/stalled), and concrete timestamp/landmark evidence. Cover the entire selected duration in order. Use the SAME sectionId for the same route section across recordings and a NEW attemptId after a reset; do not rename a failed obstacle to bypass detection. Adjacent cuts within one attempt can share IDs. Only accept material with genuinely engaging progression; unique frame counts are a separate technical check. Do not mark watchedWholeSelection true after viewing only isolated stills. The reviewer must see enough motion to assess the whole sequence.

The final background manifest retains this review and the exact derived-source hash. Historical manifests without a progression review need renewed review before new script/voice generation. This selector does not write narration, capture automatically, or publish.
