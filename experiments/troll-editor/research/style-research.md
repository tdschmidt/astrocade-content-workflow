# Troll / skull / velocity editing references

Research date: 2026-10-07. This is an evidence-backed style brief, not a claim about the most popular songs this week. Sources below were inspected as text/metadata; videos were not measured frame by frame. Exact timings in the recipes are proposed experiment defaults. A user-supplied reference would allow closer audiovisual matching.

## What is supported by sources

The recurring structure is a readable gameplay setup, a specific surprising event, then a musical and visual reaction. The strongest game-specific primary source is **coah's Phonk Edit**, a Minecraft mod explicitly recreating YouTube Shorts edits. Its creator lists greyscale freeze frames, skull images, motion blur, shake, variable speed, and portrait-style bars, with triggers based on blocks, combat, health, and airtime. [Creator project](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/phonk-edit)

**So Tuff**, another Minecraft creator project, independently specifies a black-and-white freeze frame and shaking emoji. [Creator project](https://modrinth.com/mod/so-tuff)

Editor **Ka Production** has a Roblox-specific tutorial, and its written blue-emoji tutorial explains selecting the reaction frame, freezing it, adding a face overlay, animating a quick pop/slide followed by settling, and matching the appearance to a music beat. The exact overlay can be a blue emoji, skull, or troll face. [Roblox tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-19kugRgvgs), [written workflow](https://thekaproduction.com/2025/12/14/blue-emoji-troll-face-reel-editing-capcut/)

The creator **Phonk_music_v_b** describes a Troll Face workflow using velocity, beat sync, glow, shake, zoom, and motion blur. CapCut's own guide confirms custom speed curves plus smooth slow motion. [Creator tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MNHtG39AdyU), [CapCut speed-curve guide](https://www.capcut.com/resource/how-to-do-velocity-on-capcut)

Individual creator templates establish that freeze/troll and deep-fried formats exist, but their page metadata does not prove exact effect settings or soundtrack identity. [Jay2stack's Freeze troll face](https://www.capcut.com/template-detail/Freeze-troll-face/7382814681502584070), [notworthit's Deep Fried](https://www.capcut.com/template-detail/Deep-Fried/7384186034285858054)

## Three testable styles

The following timings and parameter ranges are **our design recipes**, not measurements copied from reference videos. Choose one story beat and one dominant style per short. Timing is relative to `t0`, the first source frame on which the outcome is legible.

### 1. Freeze + troll payoff

Best for a surprising win, narrow escape, sudden high score, or an absurd collision whose outcome is already visible.

- Show 3–6 seconds of clean setup; preserve the action that explains the result.
- In the last 0.3–0.5 seconds before `t0`, reduce music and optionally slow footage to 0.6–0.75x. Do not freeze before viewers can understand the outcome.
- At `t0`, land the bass/drop, freeze for 0.45–0.8 seconds, desaturate to black and white, and punch in roughly 8–15% toward the relevant subject.
- Pop a troll/skull reaction overlay in over 0.08–0.15 seconds, settle it, and keep the character/action or score readable. Add a decaying shake for 0.15–0.3 seconds, not continuous random movement.
- Resume 0.4–0.8 seconds of the result, or use a 1–2 second short replay if it adds information. End after the payoff instead of dragging out the frozen image.
- Sound: short sub impact, bass-forward beat, retained game impact. Reference mood: slowed montage or drift-phonk drop. If using a placeholder, identify it clearly as an original synthetic cue.

This recipe translates the freeze/greyscale/emoji vocabulary documented by the Minecraft creator sources into a short editorial pattern.

### 2. Velocity clutch / beat montage

Best for a sequence of successful dodges, hits, jumps, or accelerating obstacle avoidance. Requires visible motion, not a largely static screen.

- Establish the objective and movement direction in the first 1–2 seconds.
- Cut waiting/travel, and compress uninteresting motion at 1.25–1.75x.
- Just before the decisive action, use 0.45–0.65x for 0.4–0.7 seconds of output time, then return to 1x or 1.3x on the musical hit.
- Use 2–4 subsequent beat-aligned cuts or punch-ins, separated by readable action. Use a mild motion trail or blur only around a transition.
- Keep most footage in color. Reserve a 0.15–0.3 second greyscale accent or one reaction overlay for the strongest beat; no long still unless the style intentionally crosses into recipe 1.
- Sound: continuous rhythmic phrase, with the cut/velocity changes matching real musical transients. Reference tracks: MONTAGEM XONADA / METAMORPHOSIS; choose the supplied version and map its beats.

This recipe uses the creator velocity tutorial and CapCut's documented speed-curve mechanism. The narrative rules and numeric values are experiment choices.

### 3. Deep-fried fail / fake confidence

Best for a visible near miss, failed jump, game over, or an obviously overconfident setup contradicted by the footage. Never invent a win/fail the clip does not show.

- Show 3–5 seconds of readable setup with one short caption if needed; avoid explaining the punchline first.
- At the clear failure, interrupt the music for 0.08–0.15 seconds, punch in, and apply a very short saturated/high-contrast/sharpened/noisy burst for 0.15–0.3 seconds. Keep enough detail to identify the failure.
- Follow with 0.5–0.8 seconds of a monochrome still or slow motion. Add one reaction face and a small caption only if it contributes a joke.
- Optional single replay: 0.6–1.2 seconds, with an arrow/crop identifying the missed hazard. Do not stack multiple identical replays.
- Sound: impact/scratch/downward pitch cue, then a dry, short ending or deliberately anticlimactic beat. Distort a synthesized cue before limiting its level; loudness itself is not the joke.
- Deep fry implementation can combine contrast/saturation boosts, short noise, and sharpening. Treat these as authored visual parameters; the available CapCut template verifies the format name, not these exact settings.

This third style is the least strongly verified as a Minecraft/Roblox-specific recipe; it is a deliberate adaptation of the requested deep-fried meme treatment. A user example would improve fidelity substantially.

## What the editor agent needs to decide

1. Inspect frames and audio around candidate outcomes; write the visible event and exact source time. “Interesting motion” alone is not a story.
2. Preserve enough setup for cause and effect. Select the style by event type, not by random template.
3. Write an edit decision list with source in/out points, output times, speed, crop, caption, overlay, and audio cue identifiers. Name the freeze source frame separately from its output duration.
4. Keep music and effect assets separate from the creative decision. Record provenance, version, and permitted use for each supplied asset; report missing inputs instead of silently substituting an unverified web rip.
5. Render, then inspect the actual output around every effect boundary with sound. Reject an edit if the payoff is hidden, a fake caption misstates it, audio clips, the crop loses essential information, or an effect is unrelated to an observed event.

## Audio and overlay packaging

For local experiments, bundle original synthesized rhythm/impact cues or supplied cleared files, plus cue sheets for the named reference songs. Synthetic cues test timing and mixing; they do not establish that the sample matches the commercial track's appeal. Do not describe them as the researched song or a close recreation of it.

YouTube's Shorts music picker has platform-specific agreements. YouTube says a song inserted outside its Shorts creation tools can receive standard copyright claims. A downloadable copy found in a mod, template, or soundboard is not evidence of reuse permission. [YouTube Shorts rights-holder documentation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13053317?hl=en-GB)

For a future YouTube export, either use a track whose supplied license covers that export or deliver a music-free master and an alignment cue sheet for adding the official song through the intended platform workflow. YouTube's production Audio Library is a separate option; its Creative Commons tracks can require attribution. Check the specific entry rather than labeling all Internet “royalty-free” uploads as cleared. [YouTube Audio Library documentation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882)

An original reaction-face illustration is a practical experiment asset. Label it as original, rather than implying it is the classic Trollface or that a copied meme/emoji's rights were checked. Any user-provided examples can be used as audiovisual references without bundling their downloaded music, overlays, or footage.
