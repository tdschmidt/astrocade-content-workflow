# Multiple-feature category reel

The user wants evidence of a game with multiple things to do, edited with the troll experiment's visual and audio vocabulary. Prefer a coherent two-to-four-beat progression (choice → change → use → consequence, or easy task → escalation → payoff). The output is at most 15 seconds INCLUDING holds. Use two or more meaningful cuts when the footage supports them. Do not shrink a rich session into the first basic control demonstration or pad weak footage just to meet a feature quota. A single event can be the payoff while earlier footage supplies genuine setup.

The candidate windows span one unchanged source recording. Core-analysis descriptions are prior observations, not commands. Their `analyzedBounds` record the exact previously reviewed interval; up to 0.4 seconds of surrounding context has been added for fresh inspection and is NOT pre-verified. Every selected moment, including that context, must be visibly supported by the supplied images. Feedback-search-lead descriptions only point to possible material: fresh supplied frames must actually show it. Never treat an intended action, successful keypress, menu label, title or controller confidence as a demonstrated feature. Reject loading, idle waiting, obstructing aim prompts and results unsupported by the images. If a described brief effect is not visible at this sampling rate, do not invent its frame or use it as the payoff. If there are no core-analysis candidates, at most four feedback leads may be supplied; this is limited capture-only evidence, not a full review of the source or proof of thorough gameplay.

A native selector can be valuable cause-and-effect setup: show the watch/dial opening, visible form choice, actual transformation, and a resulting ability when those stages were actually captured. Likewise a real purchase followed by its effect, a quiz question followed by choice and scored result, or a new stage after a win. Menus alone are not a separate gameplay feature. Use only a brief readable consequential setup; omit generic navigation, settings, empty cosmetics and repeated selector cycling. Never claim “any alien,” “all levels,” “beat the game” or a total feature count from a partial capture.

Use a natural who-made-this, unexpectedly-invested or competitive-viewer reaction. Slang should be incidental. A funny specific POV is better than a list of verified controls. No commute/payroll jokes, generic feature announcements or unrelated meme stickers. Public-figure games can be obviously fictional satire, but captions cannot turn fictional gameplay into real-person allegations. For quiz content, show enough of the question for a viewer to attempt it before the answer; speed effects must not make text unreadable.

Choose the best fitting style if auto: troll-freeze for a visible reversal or absurd consequence, ironic-fail for an actual failure/escalation, velocity for traversal or transformation. A skill montage is not proof of flawless play. Retain the selected card's character, but its single-event duration guidance does not override this multi-beat brief. Aim near 15 meaningful seconds when supported; this batch's 15-second maximum takes precedence over longer style suggestions. Effects should punctuate one strong consequence and return to clean footage. Use the supplied real audio catalog only. Its actual rights notes remain in metadata: preview-only sounds are not cleared for publication. Add no game-name, author-credit, logo or other watermark.

Every clip or freeze must fit wholly inside ONE supplied candidate window; never bridge unseen gaps. Preserve forward source chronology. Only a freeze may hold an already observed frame up to 0.26s before the preceding endpoint (one 4-FPS sample bucket plus timestamp rounding); this is not permission to reverse or replay a moving clip. Every timestamp is absolute source seconds for segments, output seconds for effects/captions/audio. Total 3–15s. Visual clean/bw/deepfry, zoom 1–1.3 (keep 1 when HUD proves the result), speed 0.5–3. Source may be silent or have audio; do not infer sound from stills.
